THE BIG PICTURE
------------------------------------------------------------
I took a dataset of over 1 million retail transactions and used it
to figure out which customers are the most valuable. I grouped
customers into 5 categories based on their buying behavior. This
technique is called RFM Analysis, and it is used by real companies
every day to make decisions about marketing, retention, and inventory.

My key finding: the top 20% of customers drive 77.2% of all revenue.

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4: CLEANING THE DATA
------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most important step. Real data is messy. Your job as
an analyst is to make it usable. I did 5 things here:

1. DROP ROWS WITH NO CUSTOMER ID
   Code: df = df.dropna(subset=["Customer ID"])
   If a row has no Customer ID, I cannot connect that transaction
   to a customer. It is useless for my analysis, so I remove it.
   This removed 243,007 rows.

2. REMOVE DUPLICATE ROWS
   Code: df = df.drop_duplicates()
   Sometimes data gets recorded twice by accident. Keeping duplicates
   would inflate my numbers and give us incorrect results.

3. REMOVE CANCELLED ORDERS
   Code: df = df[~df["Invoice"].astype(str).str.startswith("C")]
   In this dataset, any invoice that starts with the letter "C"
   means the order was canceled. Canceled orders should not count
   as purchases, so I filter them out. The "~" symbol means "NOT"
   which keeps everything that does NOT start with "C".

4. REMOVE NEGATIVE OR ZERO VALUES
   Code: df = df[df["Quantity"] > 0]
         df = df[df["Price"] > 0]
   Negative quantities and prices are data errors. I only keep
   rows where both values are greater than zero.

5. CREATE A TOTALPRICE COLUMN
   Code: df["TotalPrice"] = df["Quantity"] * df["Price"]
   This creates a brand new column by multiplying Quantity by Price.
   For example, if someone bought 3 items at $5 each, TotalPrice = $15.
   This is called a derived column. It did not exist in the original
   data, I calculated it.

After cleaning, I went from 1,067,371 rows down to 779,425 rows.
That is 287,946 rows removed because they were incomplete or invalid.
------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5: BUILDING THE RFM TABLE
------------------------------------------------------------
Code:
    rfm = df.groupby("Customer ID").agg(
        Recency   = ("InvoiceDate", lambda x: (reference_date - x.max()).days),
        Frequency = ("Invoice", "nunique"),
        Monetary  = ("TotalPrice", "sum")
    ).reset_index()

RFM stands for Recency, Frequency, and Monetary. It is a framework
used to measure how valuable each customer is based on three questions:

RECENCY: How recently did this customer buy something?
   A customer who bought last week is more valuable than one who
   bought two years ago. Lower number of days = better.
   I calculated this by finding each customer's most recent
   purchase date and subtracting it from a reference date
   (one day after the last transaction in the dataset).

FREQUENCY: How often does this customer buy?
   A customer who buys 20 times is more valuable than one who
   bought once. Higher number = better.
   I used "nunique" which counts the number of unique invoices
   per customer -- meaning unique shopping trips, not individual items.

MONETARY: How much money has this customer spent in total?
   A customer who spent $5,000 is more valuable than one who spent $10.
   Higher number = better.
   I used "sum" to add up all the TotalPrice values per customer.

groupby("Customer ID"), groups all 779,425 rows by customer so that
each customer ends up as a single row with their R, F, and M values.
The result is 5,878 rows which is one per unique customer.
------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 6: SCORING AND SEGMENTING CUSTOMERS
------------------------------------------------------------
Code:
    rfm["R_Score"] = pd.qcut(rfm["Recency"], q=5, labels=[5,4,3,2,1])
    rfm["F_Score"] = pd.qcut(rfm["Frequency"].rank(method="first"), q=5, labels=[1,2,3,4,5])
    rfm["M_Score"] = pd.qcut(rfm["Monetary"].rank(method="first"), q=5, labels=[1,2,3,4,5])

pd.qcut() divides customers into 5 equal groups based on their values.
q=5 means 5 groups. Each customer gets a score from 1 to 5.

For Recency, the labels are reversed [5,4,3,2,1] because a LOWER
number of days since purchase is BETTER. So the most recent customers
get a 5 and the least recent get a 1.

For Frequency and Monetary, higher values are better so labels go
[1,2,3,4,5] -- customers who buy more often and spend more get a 5.

.rank(method="first") is used to handle ties. If two customers have
the exact same Frequency, it assigns them different ranks so they
do not have both end up in the same bucket.

Then all three scores are added together:
    rfm["RFM_Score"] = R_Score + F_Score + M_Score

The maximum possible score is 15 (5+5+5) and the minimum is 3 (1+1+1).
Customers are then placed into named segments:

    Score 13-15: Champions       (your best customers)
    Score 10-12: Loyal Customers (buy regularly, spend well)
    Score  7-9:  At Risk         (used to be good, slowing down)
    Score  4-6:  Needs Attention (low engagement)
    Score  3:    Lost            (have not bought in a very long time)

My results:
    Needs Attention: 1,456 customers
    At Risk:         1,453 customers
    Loyal Customers: 1,357 customers
    Champions:       1,295 customers
    Lost:              317 customers

STEP 7: KEY INSIGHT
------------------------------------------------------------
Code:
    top_20_cutoff = rfm["Monetary"].quantile(0.80)
    top_20_revenue = rfm[rfm["Monetary"] >= top_20_cutoff]["Monetary"].sum()
    top_20_pct = (top_20_revenue / total_revenue) * 100

quantile(0.80) finds the spending value that separates the top 20%
of customers from the bottom 80%. Anyone at or above that value is
in the top 20%.

I then added up all the revenue from those top customers and divided
by total revenue to get a percentage.

Result: The top 20% of customers drive 77.2% of total revenue.

This is a real business insight. It means the company should focus
heavily on retaining its Champions and Loyal Customers because
losing them would have a massive impact on revenue.
